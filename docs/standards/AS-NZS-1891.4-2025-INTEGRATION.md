## Confirmed source identity

- AS/NZS 1891.4:2025, *Personal equipment for work at height, Part 4:
  Selection, use and maintenance*.
- Published 19 September 2025.
- Third edition; supersedes AS/NZS 1891.4:2009.
- Scope shown includes harnesses, lanyards, pole straps, connectors, anchors,
  fall-arrest devices, horizontal lifelines and horizontal rail systems.
- Scope excludes total-restraint equipment, rescue operations, emergency
  services, recreational activities and theatrical flying.
- Performance requirements are outside this document and refer to other parts
  of the AS/NZS 1891 series, AS 1891.5 and AS 5532.

## Major 2025 changes visible in supplied pages

- Additional information: fall-clearance calculations, diversion anchors,
  chemical fasteners, friction fasteners, re-anchoring and work-positioning

- Periodic inspection frequencies clarified.
- Fall-protection hierarchy clarified.

- Removed references include AS/NZS 4488.2 and withdrawn AS 4142.3.

## Requirements to model, not auto-decide

### Selection hierarchy

The supplied section 2 pages list descending priority:

1. Total restraint
2. Restrained fall
3. Limited free fall
4. Free fall
5. Restraint technique

Task assessment must capture access, prevention of access to the fall hazard,
movement around the work area, and tools/equipment interactions. Selection must
minimize free-fall distance and potential injury, and consider jurisdictional
requirements.

### System configuration

The supplied Table 2.1 identifies these candidate minimum-rating inputs:

| System | Minimum anchor/anchorage rating shown | Connecting system shown |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Total restraint | Not specified in this document | Fixed-length connection |
| Restrained fall | 12 kN | Pole strap |
| Limited free fall | 12 kN | Lanyard or fall-arrest device, Types 1–4 |
| Free fall | 15 kN | Lanyard |
| Restraint technique | 15 kN | Lanyard |

The supplied section 2.3.1 page states that systems must not allow a fall
greater than 2 m. Keep rating and configuration values as recorded evidence;
do not treat them as universal design approval.

### Additional section 2 controls reviewed

- Limited free fall: free fall no greater than 600 mm.
- Free fall: between 600 mm and 2 m; can occur vertically or on slopes where
  walking needs a handrail or hand line.
- Restraint technique: equipment must be fall-arrest rated; system must not be
  intended to carry load; risk assessment must cover roof edges, skylights,
  penetrations and incorrect manual adjustment.
- Equipment selection must consider chemicals/paint, welding, heat, sparks,
  rotating machinery, sharp edges, fragile surfaces, confined spaces,
  temperature and contamination. Fragile-surface clearance must include a fall
  through the surface.
- Work-positioning systems require additional fall protection for system
  failure. Systems must minimize free fall and swing fall.
- Equipment is inspected before and after each use. Failed equipment is
  removed from service and recorded. Equipment used to arrest a fall is removed
  from service or destroyed as required by the source procedure.
- A rescue plan is required before work starts. It must cover equipment,
  external-help call capability, all site locations, competent rescuers, first
  aid and timely rescue to limit suspension trauma. It must not rely solely on
  emergency services or the casualty.
- Dropped-object hazards require controls. Slippery/inclined surfaces require
  fall arrest where sliding toward a hazard is possible.
- Moveable platforms require reassessment after movement: clearance, swing
  fall, possibility of falling out, available anchor/rating and connecting
  system adjustability.
- Product instructions must be available to users. Equipment must carry
  permanent markings required by relevant Standards.

### Anchor and horizontal-system controls reviewed

- A competent person assesses potential anchorages using product instructions.
  If suitability is not evident, a professional engineer must assess it and
  provide written certification of capacity equivalent to attached anchor
  rating.
- Anchor placement must reduce free fall and swing fall, prevent exposure while
  connecting, support safe transitions and avoid multiple connectors on one
  attachment point.
- A drop used to extend an anchor or mobile attachment point must have
  equivalent rating to the anchor.
- HLL/HRS anchorage must sustain applicable loads; deflection must not contact
  fixed objects; swing fall must be assessed; access must not expose the user to
  a fall hazard; HLL must not be selected for work-positioning techniques.
- HLL/HRS inspection and corrosion/component complexity need explicit evidence.

### Additional anchor and harness controls reviewed

- Anchor user information must be available at the location and time of use;
  candidate delivery methods include labels, QR, RFID, NFC and installer or
  certifier reports.
- Diversion anchors reduce swing fall. A diversion above 60° requires load
  calculation or re-anchoring of the connecting system.
- Anchor slings must sustain rated load, be protected from sharp edges, use a
  basket internal angle under 120° unless product instructions specify
  otherwise, use a compatible connector and prevent slipping along the
  anchorage.
- Harness selection depends on work type and system type and must conform to
  AS/NZS 1891.1. Selection factors include rescue/self-rescue, donning/doffing,
  comfort during work and suspension, and adjustment to body shape.
- HLL/HRS, anchor and sling records need location, identification, product
  information, load basis and evidence of safe configuration.

### Harness controls reviewed

- Full-body harness fall-arrest attachment may be front chest, front waist or
  rear dorsal; additional points may support pole straps, retrieval and total
  restraint.
- Lower-body harnesses must not be used for free-fall applications. Upper-body
  assemblies must not be used alone. Combination assemblies must support the
  body, manage applied load, prevent fall-out and be compatible per product
  instructions.
- Full-body or combination harness is required where free fall above 600 mm is
  possible. Waist belts/straps alone are not acceptable for covered uses.
- Side waist points are for pole straps as a pair; centre rear waist point is
  for total restraint; retrieval points are not fall-arrest points.
- Check choking hazard, sub-pelvic strap position and final strap-fit test
  before use. Store harness type, attachment-point purpose and fit-check result.

### Connecting-system controls reviewed

- Connecting systems connect harness to anchor and limit maximum arrest force
  to less than 6 kN, except pole straps. Lanyards/pole straps follow AS 1891.5;
  fall-arrest devices follow AS 1891.3.
- Record lanyard form: single-line, adjustable, twin-line, leading-edge or
  tie-back. Record energy absorber, adjuster, stopper and connector roles.
- Type 1 devices run on a vertical rail/rope/cable within 15° either side and
  lock during a fall. Type 2 self-retracting devices extend/retract and lock;
  Type 3 adds retrieval; Type 4 adds automatic descent after arrest.
- Pole straps are for pole work where free fall cannot occur and use side-waist
  attachment points. Do not select a fall-arrest device where its locking
  function can be impeded by surface or movement conditions.
- Connecting-system selection must control task hazards, minimize free fall,
  enable rescue and permit safe transitions between systems.
- Attachment-point purpose/location must be explicit: sternal/dorsal for free
  fall or limited-free-fall rescue/restraint technique, side-waist for pole
  straps, front waist/ventral only with risk assessment, retrieval for rescue,
  rear waist/restraint for total restraint.
- Use only one energy absorber between user and anchor unless product
  instructions say otherwise. Twin-tail systems need explicit unused-tail
  stowage; do not short-circuit the absorber.
- Do not back-hook except with a tie-back assembly. Pole-strap connectors engage
  before loading; adjustable lanyards use shortest practical length. Type 1
  ladder devices use sternal or front-waist points and need a line termination.

### Connector controls reviewed

- Connector selection depends on work and system type.
- Snap hooks must be self-closing, self-locking, have 6 kN minimum gate-face
  and gate-side resistance, and require a locking latch secured by two or more
  separate manual operations.
- Record connector compatibility, opening size, operation with gloves and latch
  check result.
- Karabiners require self-closing, 6 kN gate-face and gate-side resistance,
  22.2 kN longitudinal strength and a locking mechanism. Users check manually
  locking gates before use and check self-locking gates are locked.
- Screwlink/class Q connectors conform to BS EN 362 and close per product
  instructions.
- System compatibility must cover gate loading, accidental unlocking, correct
  orientation and adequate clearance. Product-specific exceptions require
  product-instruction evidence.
- Connector checks must include rough edges, bending stress and roll-out risk
  from overload, locking failure or accidental latch activation.

### Fall-clearance controls reviewed

The supplied section 7 page gives the core check as:

`FC > A + L + E + 2.8 m`

Where A is anchor deflection, L is functional connection-system length and E
is energy-absorber extension including fall-arrest-device activation distance.
The 2.8 m allowance includes harness slippage, connection height and a safety
factor. The project must capture each input, units, source, assumptions and
reviewer; never hide the calculation inside a pass/fail label.

- Swing fall must be assessed and minimized. Candidate controls: alternative
  work system, diversion anchor, dual anchors and anchor location above or
  beside the worker.
- Free-fall distance is `FF = L − S + H`, where L is functional connection
  length, S is straight-line distance from anchor device to nearest fall hazard,
  and H is connection attachment height to the harness.
- Store both clearance and free-fall calculations; they answer different risk
  questions and must not be collapsed into one value.

### Traceability, inspection and storage controls reviewed

- Every component must be traceable to point of purchase by manufacturer
  identifier, user-installed identifier or both. Added labels must follow
  product instructions, avoid connection/performance/inspection interference
  and preserve manufacturer-label readability.
- Inspection register must retain component ID, first-use/entry date,
  remove-from-service date, inspection date and inspector, product-instruction
  evaluation, monitoring notes, next inspection date, rating, system type and
  proof-test records where applicable. Keep records for component life and
  after removal from service.
- Maximum intervals shown in Table 8.1: single-point anchors, HLL/HRS and
  chemical/friction fasteners 12 months; textile components, harnesses,
  lanyards/pole straps and fall-arrest devices 6 months. Harsh environments
  may require shorter intervals.
- Inspection checks include legible markings, manufacturer criteria and extra
  manufacturer requirements. Check anchor load rating and relevant Standard;
  harness remove-from-service date; fall-arrest contamination and Type 2–4
  lock-off; textile cuts, abrasion, stretch, indicators, threads, heat,
  chemicals, dirt and UV; metallic cracks, distortion, wire/rope damage,
  latch/moving-part function, obstruction and cross-sectional wear.
- Store synthetic equipment cool, dry, away from direct sunlight and air-dry
  at ambient temperature before storage. Service per product instructions;
  remove non-serviceable equipment.
- Remove equipment after fall arrest, failed inspection or reached
  remove-from-service date. Identify defective equipment, re-inspect by a
  competent person or destroy it.
- Chemical and friction fasteners require proof testing at installation and
  annually. Multiple-fastener exceptions require calculations, manufacturer
  installation compliance, valid warranty and lifetime installation evidence.
- Proof-test method shown: axial load only, no shear; load to 50% of specified
  design load and at least 7.5 kN; hold at least 30 seconds. Multiple-fastener
  calculations must include all forces/moments plus concrete condition,
  thickness, compressive strength and edge distance.

### Appendices reviewed

- Total restraint is distinct from restraint technique and is outside the main
  equipment scope. It prevents reaching a fall hazard, requires a sound
  surface, no harness load and no need to disconnect/reconnect to reach another
  point. It is unsuitable where a fall remains possible, through a surface or
  after platform failure.
- Recommended training outcomes include legislation and Standards knowledge,
  rescue equipment and plans, equipment selection/fitting/adjustment/use,
  product instructions, pre-use inspection, anchor identification, dropped
  object controls and suspension-intolerance first aid.
- Management training includes system and task selection, rescue plans,
  worker training, dropped-object methods, safe work method statements,
  control monitoring and inspection/maintenance management.
- Periodic-inspection training covers textile, metallic, energy absorber and
  Type 1–4 devices; system-inspection training covers anchors, rails, HLL,
  Type 1 devices, anchorages and fasteners, including calibration, tagging,
  reports and removal-from-service decisions.
- Swing-fall review must consider objects below the edge, swing-path hazards,
  snagging/obstruction risk and increased clearance. A lanyard in tension does
  not prove restraint if another edge remains within reach.
