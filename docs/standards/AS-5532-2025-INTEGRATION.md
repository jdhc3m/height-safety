## Standard identity and boundary

AS 5532:2025 is titled **Manufacturing requirements for single-point anchor
device used for harness-based work at height**. The supplied title page states
that it was published on 19 September 2025, originated as AS/NZS 5532:2013,
and was revised and redesignated as AS 5532:2025.

The standard specifies manufacturing performance requirements, test methods,
marking and product instructions for single-point anchor devices used with the
AS/AS-NZS 1891 series. It covers fixed and portable anchor devices. It does
not perform the site assessment, overall system/layout selection, onsite
proof-testing or anchorage inspection that the project must manage separately.
It also excludes horizontal lifeline and rail systems, vertical lifeline
systems, installation/layout/use guidance, pole-top rescue, theatrical flying,
total restraint, rescue operations and emergency-services use.

For this project, AS 5532 evidence describes the product and its manufacture;
AS/NZS 1891.4 evidence remains necessary for selection, use, inspection,
maintenance, compatibility, traceability, installation location and removal
from service.

## Test evidence to capture

For every test record the specimen, procedure, result and clause reference,
deviations, unusual observations, test date and the dated designation
`AS 5532:2025`.

### Apparatus and configuration

- Force measurement: SAE J211 Channel Frequency Class 60; minimum sample rate
  1,000 Hz.
- Static loading: crosshead 150 mm/min ±20 mm/min to the applicable rating
  plus 0.5 kN / minus 0 kN; alternate loading from initial onset to that force
  over 60 s ±3 s.
- Rigid structure: 20 kN applied at any point causes no more than 1 mm
  deflection; the test mass must not contact the floor or another object.
- Test mass: rigid 100 kg ±1 kg, with a rigid central connection point.
- Test lanyard: 11 mm EN 1891 Type A static kernmantle rope, 2 m ±0.05 m
  between connector bearing surfaces, with sewn eyes.
- Extension cable where used: 7x19 stainless-steel cable, 8 mm ±1 mm,
  grade 302/304/316, eye at each end.
- Record roof/purlin/rafter/sheeting/batten/top-hat/concrete details required
  by the product instructions and the test configuration.

### Required procedures and results

Record each permitted load direction, the weakest/least stable product
configuration, the weakest/least stable instructed anchorage, and whether the
test mass remained suspended. Do not use diversion pulleys in static/dynamic
tests unless they are part of the device under test.

Capture static test force and at least 3-minute hold time. Capture dynamic
peak load, drop height, mass count and suspension result; keep each mass within
300 mm horizontally of the attachment point. Two-person tests attach the
second lanyard/mass after the first mass is suspended and prevent collision.

For flexible roof or field-anchorage configurations, capture the function test
and the complete roof structure used. For portable-anchor SRL mounting
brackets, capture the extension cable, redirect pulleys, test lanyard, mass,
limited-free-fall drop height and roof structure.

## Instructions and marking

Product instructions may be physical or electronic and must include:

- direction to AS/NZS 1891.4 for selection, use and maintenance;
- correct installation and use, anchor rating, personnel-only use and product
  limitations;
- applicable temperature range, product life, bimetallic-corrosion warning,
  connecting-equipment restrictions and corrosive-environment restrictions;
- warning against alterations/additions without written manufacturer consent;
- directions to AS/NZS 1891.4 for traceability, multiple-anchor-system design,
  installation location, visual pre-use inspection, connector compatibility,
  inspection frequency and removal from service; and
- a dated reference to AS 5532:2025.

Each product must be clearly, indelibly and permanently marked, with marking
remaining legible after installation, showing manufacturer/supplier or other
identification, batch or serial number, anchor rating, personnel-attachment
only indication and `AS 5532:2025`.

Appendix A is informative. Its additional design recommendations include
adequate thread engagement, sufficiently sized/thick through-anchor
back-plates, future inspection access for permanently installed devices,
avoidance of adverse chemical reactions such as bimetallic corrosion, suitable
feature radii, and minimised bending/shear on threaded components.

## Project changes

The project should provide:

1. a product-evidence record linked to each installed anchor;
2. structured capture for rating, person count, anchor class, test procedure,
   apparatus, configuration, load, drop, peak load, hold time, suspension,
   roof structure, deviations and dated standard reference;
3. instruction and marking checklists with missing-evidence flags;
4. a clear report distinction between AS 5532 manufacturing evidence and
   AS/NZS 1891.4 site selection/inspection/maintenance evidence; and
5. review status and source provenance for every rule-backed assessment.

Do not label an anchor compliant from AS 5532 screenshots alone. The report
must expose missing product identity, installation, structural, compatibility,
inspection or site-specific evidence and route unresolved technical decisions
to the authorised inspector or professional engineer.
