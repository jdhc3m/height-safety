# G-06 research — retention, export, backups and deletion

**Research date:** 2026-10-09

**Status:** Research note informing D-051's synthetic-only policy. It does not close G-06 or approve real-data use.

**Scope:** Australian privacy and records guidance relevant to the product's NSW/Queensland private-company workflow, plus public first-party retention/export/backup/deletion statements from two inspection/field-work software vendors. No roof-anchor legal retention rule or standards interpretation is inferred.

## Executive finding

- **[Statute/regulator guidance — high confidence on the cited rule; applicability is case-specific]** The Commonwealth Privacy Act does not prescribe one general retention period or one backup interval for every private business. Where the Australian Privacy Principles (APPs) apply, APP 11 requires reasonable security steps and reasonable steps to destroy or de-identify personal information when it is no longer needed for any permitted purpose, subject to Commonwealth-record and law/court-order exceptions. OAIC guidance explicitly includes archived and backup copies in the destruction question. ([Privacy Act 1988, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A03712/latest/text); [OAIC APP 11, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-11-app-11-security-of-personal-information))
- **[Regulator guidance — high confidence]** APP 12 is an individual’s right to access their personal information, not a general right for a company to receive a complete, machine-readable export of all business records. Public-sector records duties and GIPA/RTI/FOI access processes are separate. ([OAIC APP 12, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-12-app-12-access-to-personal-information); [NSW State Records NSW retention/disposal FAQ, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.nsw.gov.au/nsw-government/recordkeeping/sentence-and-dispose/retention-and-disposal-of-records-faq); [Queensland State Archives public-records guidance, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.forgov.qld.gov.au/information-technology/recordkeeping-and-information-management/recordkeeping/disposal-of-records))
- **[Regulator guidance — high confidence]** NSW and Queensland public-sector recordkeeping has separate records legislation and record-class retention/disposal instruments. The applicable schedule and legal holds can require continued retention even where a privacy principle would otherwise favour destruction. These are public-sector duties, not a default retention schedule for a private roof-anchor inspection company. ([NSW State Records NSW authorities, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.nsw.gov.au/nsw-government/recordkeeping/records-retention-and-disposal-authorities); [Queensland Public Records Act guidance, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.forgov.qld.gov.au/information-technology/recordkeeping-and-information-management/recordkeeping/public-records-act))
- **[Vendor statement — medium confidence as to the public claim; not independently verified]** The two vendors publish usable export and deletion workflows, but their statements differ and leave gaps. Mitti (by SafetyCulture) says it performs regular encrypted backups but publishes no schedule/window on the reviewed security page; its current terms also make the customer responsible for backups and require data export before subscription end. Fulcrum states nightly database backups are retained for 90 days. Separately, Fulcrum's retention-policy matrix says customer-collected electronic data stays in active storage for 90 days and then in tiered offline backups for another 90 days after a customer is no longer an Active customer; it says a script is run to purge the database. These are vendor policy statements, not independent verification of a specific customer's configuration or deletion across every data copy. ([Mitti Security, accessed 2026-10-09](https://mitti.com/security); [Mitti Terms, effective 2026-09-21, accessed 2026-10-09](https://mitti.com/legal/terms-and-conditions); [Fulcrum Security and Compliance, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.fulcrumapp.com/security/); [Fulcrum Data Retention and Disposal Policy, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.fulcrumapp.com/data-retention-and-disposal-policy-and-procedures/); [Fulcrum retention-policy PDF, matrix, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.fulcrumapp.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Data-Retention-Destruction-Policy.pdf))
- **[Project decision D-051 — user-directed, synthetic-only]** Keep D-018’s no-automatic-deletion posture for server inspection evidence, reports and audit history; permit only an explicit reset of a wholly synthetic tenant after a tenant-scoped export is available and verified, with actor, time, scope and reason recorded. Encrypt and inventory any persistent synthetic-development backup and expire it within 30 days. Do not use real or customer-derived data until G-06 receives owner plus legal/privacy review. The 30-day cap is a project choice for synthetic backups, not a legal requirement, vendor benchmark or real-data schedule. ## What Australian privacy and records guidance requires

### 1. Commonwealth Privacy Act and OAIC guidance

- **[Statute/regulator guidance — high confidence; coverage must be checked]** The Privacy Act applies to Australian Government agencies and many private organisations, including organisations over the $3 million annual-turnover threshold, plus specified small-business exceptions. The OAIC also identifies situations where employee-record handling is exempt. A small inspection business should not assume either that it is covered or that an exemption resolves all customer, worker, contractor, photograph or service-provider data. ([OAIC, The Privacy Act, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-legislation/the-privacy-act); [OAIC, Rights and responsibilities, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-legislation/the-privacy-act/rights-and-responsibilities); [OAIC APP Guidelines, Chapter B, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-b-key-concepts))
- **[Regulator guidance — high confidence]** APP 11.1 requires reasonable steps to protect personal information an APP entity holds from misuse, interference, loss and unauthorized access, modification or disclosure. OAIC says “reasonable” depends on such factors as amount/sensitivity, possible harm, business model and third-party/cloud handling. It gives inadequate backup after a system failure as an example of electronic loss. The guidance does not set a universal backup frequency, RPO, backup lifetime or number of copies. ([OAIC APP 11, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-11-app-11-security-of-personal-information))
- **[Statute/regulator guidance — high confidence]** Under APP 11.2, an APP entity must take reasonable steps to destroy or de-identify personal information once it is no longer needed for any purpose for which it may be used or disclosed under the APPs, except where it is contained in a Commonwealth record or an Australian law or court/tribunal order requires retention. OAIC guidance says organisations should address copies they hold, including archived and backup copies. If immediate irretrievable deletion is technically impossible, “beyond use” is a limited-case route with controls including no use/disclosure, no access by others, appropriate security with logs/audit trails, and a commitment to destroy when feasible. For cloud copies, OAIC says reasonable steps include verifying the provider’s instructed destruction. ([Privacy Act 1988, Schedule 1 APP 11, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A03712/latest/text); [OAIC APP 11, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-11-app-11-security-of-personal-information))
- **[Inference from regulator guidance — high confidence]** APP 11 gives a purpose-/context-based duty, not a blanket “keep everything forever” or “delete every item immediately” rule. A record-class schedule must account for lawful purpose, other retention duties, copies/backups and the evidence needed to verify deletion. ([OAIC APP 11, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-11-app-11-security-of-personal-information))
- **[Regulator guidance — high confidence]** APP 12 gives an individual access to personal information about that individual on request. It does not create a general entitlement to all non-personal company records or a tenant-wide business archive. Organisations must respond within a reasonable period; OAIC’s general guide is no more than 30 calendar days. Access should be given in the requested manner when reasonable and practicable. Treat this as a privacy-access workflow separate from a customer’s contractual business export. ([OAIC APP 12, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-12-app-12-access-to-personal-information))
- **[Regulator guidance — high confidence]** APP 13 requires reasonable steps to correct personal information that is inaccurate, outdated, incomplete, irrelevant or misleading for the purpose for which it is held. If correction is refused, an individual may request an associated statement. That supports a traceable correction/annotation path; it does not by itself decide whether a submitted inspection record or draft report should be overwritten. ([OAIC APP 13, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-13-app-13-correction-of-personal-information))
- **[Regulator guidance — high confidence]** For an APP entity, APP 8 and section 16C address disclosure of personal information to an overseas recipient through reasonable safeguards and accountability. OAIC distinguishes disclosure from some limited cloud-storage arrangements where the Australian entity retains effective control. That analysis is about the actual recipient, access and contract, not simply the physical region label. ([OAIC APP 8, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-8-app-8-cross-border-disclosure-of-personal-information))
- **[Inference — medium confidence]** An inspection record is not automatically personal information just because it concerns an anchor or site; it may contain personal information through inspector/reviewer details, identifiable people in photos, or information about an identifiable worker. The classification depends on whether a person is identified or reasonably identifiable and should be assessed for each data class. ([OAIC APP Guidelines, Chapter B, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-b-key-concepts); [repository field scope, checked 2026-10-09](../product/CURRENT_SCOPE.md))

### 2. NSW public-sector privacy and records duties

- **[Regulator guidance — high confidence]** The NSW Information and Privacy Commission says the PPIP Act covers NSW public-sector agencies, including government agencies, local councils and universities. Its current IPP fact sheet summarizes IPP 5: agencies must store personal information securely, keep it no longer than necessary for the lawful collection purpose, dispose of it appropriately, and protect it from loss and unauthorized access, use, modification or disclosure. This is not a general private-sector schedule. ([IPC NSW, NSW Privacy Laws, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.ipc.nsw.gov.au/privacy/nsw-privacy-laws); [IPC NSW, Information Protection Principles, August 2026, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.ipc.nsw.gov.au/resources/fact-sheet-information-protection-principles-ipps); [Privacy and Personal Information Protection Act 1998 (NSW), accessed 2026-10-09](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-1998-133))
- **[Statute/regulator guidance — high confidence]** The State Records Act 1998 applies to defined NSW “public offices,” including government bodies, councils, universities and other listed bodies; its definition generally excludes private-sector entities except to the extent the Act specifies. State Records NSW says retention and disposal authorities (RDAs) identify required State archives and authorize destruction of other classes after minimum periods. The RDA does not compel destruction, and a public office must not dispose of records it knows may be required for possible legal action. ([State Records Act 1998 (NSW), accessed 2026-10-09](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-1998-017); [State Records NSW, public offices, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.nsw.gov.au/nsw-government/recordkeeping/legislation-and-compliance/public-offices-under-state-records-act-1998); [State Records NSW, RDAs, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.nsw.gov.au/nsw-government/recordkeeping/records-retention-and-disposal-authorities); [State Records NSW, retention/disposal FAQ, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.nsw.gov.au/nsw-government/recordkeeping/sentence-and-dispose/retention-and-disposal-of-records-faq))
- **[Regulator guidance — high confidence]** State Records NSW states there is no single retention source or blanket email period: retention depends on the business activity and the applicable RDA. It tells private organisations to consult relevant legislation, regulations and standards and seek legal advice if unsure. A private contractor providing NSW Government services must address record creation, management/access and disposal requirements in its contract or agreement. ([State Records NSW, retention/disposal FAQ, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.nsw.gov.au/nsw-government/recordkeeping/sentence-and-dispose/retention-and-disposal-of-records-faq))

### 3. Queensland public-sector privacy and records duties

- **[Statute — high confidence; coverage must be checked]** The current Queensland Information Privacy Act 2009 requires Queensland agencies to comply with the Queensland Privacy Principles (QPPs). QPP 11.1 requires reasonable protection against misuse, interference, loss and unauthorized access/modification/disclosure. QPP 11.2 requires reasonable steps to destroy or de-identify personal information no longer needed for a permitted purpose when it is not a public record and no Australian law or court/tribunal order requires retention. QPP 12 and QPP 13 cover access and correction. ([Information Privacy Act 2009 (Qld), including Schedule 3 and sections 27, 33–36, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2009-014); [OAIC state and territory privacy legislation overview, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-legislation/state-and-territory-privacy-legislation))
- **[Statute — high confidence]** Where a Queensland agency’s service arrangement involves a contractor handling personal information, sections 35–36 require the agency to take reasonable steps to bind the contracted service provider to relevant QPP obligations; a bound provider’s duties continue for information it still holds after the service arrangement ends. Section 33 separately regulates an agency’s disclosure of personal information outside Australia. ([Information Privacy Act 2009 (Qld), sections 33–36, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2009-014))
- **[Statute/regulator guidance — high confidence]** Queensland’s Public Records Act 2023 commenced on 5 December 2024. Queensland State Archives says public authorities manage public records as complete and reliable records for minimum periods set by retention and disposal schedules; schedule choice depends on legal, community and business requirements, and authorities should consult records, legal and RTI staff before disposal. Current QSA guidance says existing schedules and disposal authorizations continued under the new Act. ([Public Records Act 2023 (Qld), accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2023-033); [Queensland State Archives, Public Records Act, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.forgov.qld.gov.au/information-technology/recordkeeping-and-information-management/recordkeeping/public-records-act); [Queensland State Archives, retention/disposal, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.forgov.qld.gov.au/information-technology/recordkeeping-and-information-management/recordkeeping/disposal-of-records); [Queensland State Archives, get started, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.forgov.qld.gov.au/information-technology/recordkeeping-and-information-management/recordkeeping/get-started-with-records-management))
- **[Inference — high confidence]** A NSW or Queensland public-sector deployment cannot simply inherit a private customer’s deletion timer. Determine whether each record is personal information, a public/State record, a record covered by a particular schedule, subject to a hold, or a contractor-held record governed by the agency agreement. State jurisdiction by itself does not answer those questions. ([State Records NSW RDA FAQ, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.nsw.gov.au/nsw-government/recordkeeping/sentence-and-dispose/retention-and-disposal-of-records-faq); [Queensland State Archives, retention/disposal, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.forgov.qld.gov.au/information-technology/recordkeeping-and-information-management/recordkeeping/disposal-of-records); [repository jurisdiction boundary, checked 2026-10-09](../product/CURRENT_SCOPE.md))

### 4. Practical boundary between law and product policy

- **[Synthesis — high confidence within sources reviewed]** The sources establish purpose-based privacy duties, access/correction duties, public-sector recordkeeping and schedule-based retention. They do **not** establish a universal Australian retention duration for private inspection reports, photos, roof-anchor registers, or audit records; a mandatory 90-day backup policy; or a universal immediate-hard-delete rule. Record-specific laws, contracts, public-sector schedules and legal holds may change the answer. ([OAIC APP 11, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-11-app-11-security-of-personal-information); [State Records NSW retention/disposal FAQ, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.nsw.gov.au/nsw-government/recordkeeping/sentence-and-dispose/retention-and-disposal-of-records-faq); [Queensland State Archives retention/disposal, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.forgov.qld.gov.au/information-technology/recordkeeping-and-information-management/recordkeeping/disposal-of-records))
- **[Synthesis — high confidence within sources reviewed]** APP 12 access, a whole-company operational export, a public-sector records access request, an infrastructure backup and a permanent-deletion request are distinct operations. A product export should therefore be defined by customer agreement and product contract; it must not be inferred from APP 12 or a cloud snapshot. ([OAIC APP 12, accessed 2026-10-09](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-12-app-12-access-to-personal-information); [repository G-03 provider research, checked 2026-10-09](G-03_PROVIDER_RESEARCH.md))
- **[Scope limit — high confidence]** This note does not identify or infer any NSW/Queensland roof-anchor inspection, work-health-and-safety, product-standard, insurance or professional record-retention rule. It does not decide whether an individual partner, insurer, public authority or statutory scheme imposes one.

### Comparison and implication

- **[Synthesis — medium confidence; two vendors only]** Both vendors describe ways to export inspection data and delete records, but the published terms differ: Fulcrum gives a 90-day nightly database-backup period and a separate 90-day active-storage plus 90-day tiered-offline-backup period for customer-collected data after a customer is no longer active; Mitti says backups are regular and encrypted but leaves the period unspecified and places backup responsibility on the customer. Neither inspected source is enough to prove that export is complete for this product's revisions, audit events and associated media, or that deletion has reached all replicas, backup snapshots and subprocessors.
